# Santiago act · design outline (draft for Brandon's approval)

Status: proposal only, nothing built. All Mapudungun names below marked ⚠ are placeholders for Brandon to replace or confirm.

## Where it sits in the story
- Today: after Ti Fütra Ngoymatulfe **and** all secrets, the kuku opens the wülngiñ to Santiago: 4 maps, five small favours (st1–st5), then Yewen in the población cancha.
- Proposal: open the portal right after Ti Fütra Ngoymatulfe (secrets stay optional), so most players reach Santiago. The kuku's line changes from "now you know it all" to "the road does not end here".
- The act ends with Yewen, as now. Yewen still summons the final-act sorrows (wenangkun, illkun, llukalfe, ayentufe, profe).

## The idea
The language is not only lost in the city; it is worn down by things that are not hatred. Three pressures, each with its own district, puzzle set and warden, none of them repeating the school, mockery or shame of the final act:

| District | Pressure on the language | Warden (boss) |
|---|---|---|
| A. La feria y la radio | **Noise**: every sign, screen and speaker is in castellano; Mapudungun has no space in public | ⚠ a static/loudspeaker creature, "the noise" |
| B. El Metro (dungeon) | **Erasure**: the names of the land are still there (Pudahuel, Peñalolén, Ñuñoa, Quilicura, Macul…) but nobody knows what they mean | ⚠ a grey creature that paints over signs |
| C. Los blocks | **No time**: rushed families, grandparents far away, the chain from kuku to child breaking at home | ⚠ a clock or turnstile creature, "the hurry" |
| Población cancha | Shame | Yewen (existing) |

Order: A and C in either order, the Metro last (it connects them and leads to the población).

## Maps (about 10 new, reusing the city tiles)
1. Feria (open market): stalls, prices.
2. Radio Mapudungun booth (interior).
3. Block of flats: courtyard + two apartments (the family, the grandmother).
4. Metro station concourse (hub).
5–8. Metro dungeon: four levels (platforms, maintenance tunnels, a flooded abandoned line, the warden's depot).
9. Rooftop / mirador over the city (reward view; the names of the land seen from above).
Plus the existing plaza, calle, población and urban ruka.

## Puzzles and activities (all reuse grammar the player already has)
- **Feria prices**: big numbers in Mapudungun (mari, pataka, ⚠ waranka 1000) to pay and give change. Builds on the Nagche lesson and trafkintu.
- **Tune the radio**: dial between stations; only the one with the correctly formed sentence (person ending, -la-, -a-) comes in clearly. Fixing it gives the radio host back her voice for the warden fight.
- **Station names (Metro)**: each station is a Santiago place name from Mapudungun. To ride the right train, pick the meaning. ⚠ Brandon supplies the etymologies; I will not invent them.
- **Turnstiles**: open with the verb builder (root + -la-/-a-/-ke- + person).
- **Restoring signs**: the warden has painted over station signs; rebuild them word by word, which then lights the tunnel.
- **Dinner at home (blocks)**: help a family say things at the table in Mapudungun (kinship terms, food, imperatives: "Iyange!", "Küpange!"), so the grandmother hears it from the children.
- **The grandmother's phone call**: a short nütramkan with the kuku back in Wallmapu, by phone (the two homes connected).

- A painted Metro battle backdrop.

## Brandon's answers (Nov 23)
1. Open Santiago right after Ti Fütra Ngoymatulfe. Brandon will design a separate new level for "all secrets".
2. Noise is kept: **ngoyngoyün** ('hacer ruido confuso, p. ej. mucha gente reunida'). Erasure and "no time at home" are out; replacements proposed below.
3. Warden names and act title: Brandon, once the other two pressures are settled.
4. Place names: no fixed etymologies. The player **builds names** from words learned in Santiago; if the name is grammatically sound, it sticks (shows on signs and banners in that save).

## Replacement pressures (proposed)
- **Dispersion**: speakers scattered across a huge city, each thinking they are the only one. Fits the Metro: each station has a speaker to find and connect; the warden keeps them apart.
- **Display only** (folklorization): Mapudungun on signs, menus and souvenirs, used for "mari mari" and nothing more. The warden freezes words into decorations; you beat it by holding real conversations.
- Rejected on purpose: any boss that treats loanwords or code-mixing as the enemy (purism).

## Naming feature (draft mechanics, rules to be supplied by Brandon)
- At unnamed places (a station, a plaza, a canal, a hill in a park) the player picks parts from words they know.
- Candidate patterns: modifier + head noun (e.g. colour/size + ko/leufu/mawida/kura); verb root + -we 'place for' (as in paliwe); noun + mapu.
- A checker accepts or rejects by Brandon's rules; accepted names are saved and appear on that place's sign, banner and in the quest log.
